NBA LEAGUE PASS

From game-night utility to daily habit

League Pass is the world's NBA streaming service — but most subscribers only open it when their team is live. This is a retention redesign for its highest-churn tier: an integrated, video-first product that turns occasional viewers into daily fans.

The Problem

A frequency problem
wearing a churn problem's clothes

League Pass has ~3M subscribers, but the Standard tier — roughly 70% of the base — behaves like a game-night-only utility. People think about the NBA all day and open the app twice a week. The retention problem is, at its root, a frequency problem.

Research & Method

We let the data pick the fight

Before touching a wireframe, we sized the segments against revenue and churn, then pressure-tested three unmet-need hypotheses with real subscribers.

Quantitative

134-subscriber survey

Recruited on Prolific (Feb 2026). Segmented by tier — 69% Standard, 18% Premium — and cross-cut for re-subscription intent, feature discovery, and weekly usage.

Qualitative

In-depth interviews

One-on-one sessions with out-of-market diehards, casual bettors, and lapsed season-ticket holders — surfacing the "second-screen" behavior the survey only hinted at.

Partner data

NBA cohort analysis

The NBA product team shared weekly signup trends and new-user retention cohorts — which is how the first-few-weeks drop-off cliff surfaced.

"A Heat fan in Houston and a Cavs fan in Cleveland should open the same app and see two genuinely different experiences."
Value proposition · PRD §2
The Target User

One tier, three fans

We focused on Standard tier — the largest revenue bucket, the highest churn risk, and the lowest feature discovery. Three personas anchored the work; the out-of-market diehard led.

What We Validated

Three unmet needs, all confirmed

Each hypothesis was pre-registered, then tested. All three held.

01

The game-night-only gap High

Subscribers think about the NBA all day but use League Pass only for live games — then jump to other apps for everything in between.

Proof: 96% use it mainly for live games; the average fan uses ~3 other NBA apps.
02

Passive viewing & undiscovered value High

Subscribers want more than a passive stream, but never discover the features League Pass already has — so the subscription feels like "just live games."

Proof: 60%+ never use Replays or Stats; 36% open the app 0–2× a week.
03

The superfan identity gap Medium

Premium's differentiators (4K, no ads, concurrent streams) don't match Standard subscribers' actual pains — so loyal fans feel unrecognized rather than upgraded.

Signal: existing NBA ID exists but "doesn't deliver much value to fans."

Explicit non-goal

Blackouts

The single most-cited complaint — and deliberately out of scope. Blackouts are set by broadcast-rights deals outside the product team's control; chasing them would burn the sprint on something we couldn't ship.

Explicit non-goal

Becoming a multi-sport hub

We positioned League Pass as NBA-specific depth, not breadth. Competing with ESPN or YouTube TV on coverage dilutes the one thing League Pass can own: the daily NBA fan.

The Solution

Four features.
One habit loop.

No single feature solves retention. Together they construct the loop League Pass is missing — a reason to open the app in the morning, during the day, and at game time. Each screen below is a live, interactive prototype.

01

Video-First Home Screen

The generic, article-heavy home is gone. When your team is live, the app auto-plays the muted broadcast the instant you open it — no more three-to-five taps to the game. When nothing's on, the same surface becomes a personalized daily feed: your team's highlights, news, and a next-game countdown. Think TikTok — content is already playing.

Auto-play broadcastLive stats & social overlaysPost-game keep-watching

02

Personalized Onboarding

A short guided setup captures favorite team, up to three players, notification preferences, and content-format preference (full, condensed, or highlights) — then offers a calendar invite to the next game as a built-in, 48-hour reason to return. It seeds the personalization every other surface depends on.

03

Personalized Morning Briefing

A ~60-second, push-triggered morning digest — last night's scores, the top plays, tonight's schedule — delivered around 7:30 AM local and deep-linking straight into the app. This is the primary habit lever: it converts the morning sports-check that ESPN and Google own today into a League Pass session, moving weekly opens from 1–2 to 3–4.

04

Close-Game Smart Alerts

A notification fired by live game state — your team or player within 5 points and under 3 minutes to play — that deep-links straight into the broadcast, bypassing the home screen entirely. It turns "no game tonight" into dwell time and captures the intent that otherwise leaks to Twitter and Reddit. You can see it live inside the game prototype at the top of this page.

Product Decisions

Why we built it this way

An integrated loop, not a feature list

We resisted shipping four standalone features. Onboarding seeds the personalization the home screen renders; the briefing and alerts drive traffic back to it. Decoupled, none of them move retention.

Habit over novelty

Predictions, chat, and voting all tested as fun — and all failed the one test that mattered: do they give a reason to come back tomorrow? We weighted habit formation at 30% on purpose.

Ship inside the constraints

The only new third-party dependency is a calendar invite (EventKit / Google Calendar). No new payment or auth surface — keeping the MVP feasible for a preseason Beta.

What's Next

The roadmap

The MVP is the loop. Everything after it deepens identity and habit without re-litigating the core bet.

Now
  • Video-first home — auto-play + daily feed
  • Personalized onboarding — team, players, calendar hook
  • Morning briefing — 60-sec push digest
  • Close-game alerts — deep-link to broadcast
  • Persistent session — no re-auth for 90 days
Next
  • NBA ID streak nudge — reward the daily habit
  • TikTok-style highlight swipe — vertical discovery
  • Fantasy roster integration — a second daily reason
Later
  • Jumbotron / behind-the-scenes feed — Premium preview & upsell lever
  • Localized habit loop — the playbook for international launch markets
